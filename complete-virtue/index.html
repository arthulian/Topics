A framework for character

Complete Virtue

Belief Evaluation

  • Test each premise against the intellectual golden rule (an empirically impartial standard applied proportionally to all), ensuring that there is alignment between each inference and objective evidence.

Conduct Accountability

  • Audit your motives against an impartial standard, applying it to all parties in proportion to what is due to each, honoring its purpose over its letter; then own the consequences of your actions in proportion to your agency.

Interoceptive Decentering

  • Treat bodily sensations as physiological data, allowing them to exist in the body (their proper place) without letting your appraisal about them spiral into assumptions driven by unchecked emotions.
  • Interpose a personal boundary/space between impulse and action, so that the rational intellect has time to deliberate and judge.
  • Recognize defensive compulsions (caused by fear-of-abandonment) as submissive compliance in disguise.

Acceptance and Recovery

  • Let go of the pretense of invulnerability to recognize that our interdependence demands mutual cooperation
  • Treat planned recovery and rest as essential maintenance needed for sustained performance.

Stress Responses

  • Under acute or overwhelming pressure, set healthy boundaries and disengage. Protecting yourself is not avoidance.
  • Under mild or moderate pressure: use controlled exposure techniques to reframe risk as manageable challenge and expand your tolerance window.

Self-Worth

  • Your human dignity rests on your capacity for moral excellence; and your disposition toward it is how you honor it.
  • Recognize defensive self-sufficiency as counterdependence (posing strength due to fear of vulnerability).
  • Develop a growth mindset by viewing setbacks as skill-deficit feedback rather than as personal flaws. Setbacks don't constitute grounds for self-condemnation.

Self-Confidence Loop

  1. A Internalize achievements, stemming from deliberate effort, to build confidence in your capabilities.
  2. B Proportion confidence to your actual level of capability.
  3. C Update confidence when new evidence indicates a change in your capabilities.
  4. D Use your increased confidence to take on progressively more challenging tasks.

Situational Clarity

  • After forming a mental representation (via your perceptions), pause and use semifactual "even if" simulations to distinguish objective facts from false presuppositions.

Strategic Focus

  • During cognitive overload, set aside nonessential tasks to refocus on basic needs and prevent future burnout.
  • Avoid the illusion of complete control; recognize that some situations are contingent on external forces and are inherently unpredictable.
  • Don't become attached to sunk costs or let them influence future decisions.

Sustainable Perseverance

  • Perseverance is tempered by courage and sustained by the mercy of self-acceptance, so that it can bend without breaking.
  • Let your underlying purpose (the why) shape your goals and methods (what and how), without becoming attached to specific outcomes or risking the disappointment of sunk costs.
  • Build resilience through gradual, controlled exposure to stressors.
  • Remain committed to long-term purpose and values while adapting strategies as needed.

Glossary

TermDefinition
Intellectual golden ruleHold your own beliefs and claims to the evidence-based standard you would demand of a claim you are motivated to reject.
CounterdependenceDefensive self-sufficiency; strength performed as armor against vulnerability, rather than genuine independence.
